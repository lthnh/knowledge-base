The most imporant thing from transmission line theory is reflection coefficient. From it, everything can be calculated.

Smith chart is a visualization of reflection coefficient.

Impedance of each point on the line is represent by

$$
Z = Z_0 \dfrac{1+\Gamma}{1-\Gamma}
$$
We consider normalize the impedance by dividing it with characteristic impedance $Z_0$.

$$
z = \dfrac{Z}{Z_0} = \dfrac{1+\Gamma}{1-\Gamma} = r +jx
$$

with $\Gamma = \mathrm{Re}(\Gamma) + \mathrm{Im}(\Gamma)$.

With this equation, we can derive the following by matching $r$ with the real part of $z$:

$$
\left[ \mathrm{Re}(\Gamma) - \dfrac{r}{1+r} \right]^2 + \mathrm{Im}^2(\Gamma) = 
\left( \dfrac{1}{1+r} \right)^2
$$

This equation represents a circle with its center at $\left( \dfrac{r}{1+r}, 0 \right)$ with a radius of $\dfrac{1}{1+r}$.

If we match $x$ with the imaginary part of $z$, we have:

$$
(\mathrm{Re}(\Gamma)-1)^2 + \left[ \mathrm{Im}(\Gamma) - \dfrac{1}{x}\right]^2 = \dfrac{1}{x^2}
$$

This equation also represent a circle with its center at $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{x}\right)$ with a radius of $\dfrac{1}{x}$.

These two equations together form the Smith chart. There are several special points on the Smith chart that represent shortening load, matching load, open load, etc.

Conductance is represented as

$$
y = g + jb
$$

To represent conductance on Smith chart, we find that conductance use the same form of equation as impedance $\Gamma = -\dfrac{y-1}{y+1}$ but with opposite sign for $\Gamma$. Hence on Smith chart without conductance graph, we can take the opposite of $\Gamma$ to find appropriate parameters.

The right most point on the constant $\Gamma$ circle represents maximum impedance hence maximum voltage node. The left most point represents minimum impedance hence minimum voltage node.

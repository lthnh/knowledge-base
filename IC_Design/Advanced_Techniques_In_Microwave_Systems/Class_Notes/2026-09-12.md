## Transmission Line Analysis
Electric and magnetic fields generated from the transmission are modeled through capacitor bridging the upper and lower transmission line and inductor as lumped element on a single line.
At higher frequencies, skin effect causes conductor surface area decrease thus increase impedance $\rightarrow$ loss is greater at higher frequencies.
$G$ is shunt conduction loss due to current leakage caused by imperfect dielectric separating conductors.
## Wave Equations
Applying Kirchoff to an element of the transmission lines resulted in wave equations.
Solving the wave equations reveals wave components: incident wave and reflected wave. This is from the voltage equation.
### Propagation Parameters
Propagation constant is $\gamma(\omega)$.

$$
\gamma(\omega) = \alpha(\omega) + j\beta(\omega)
$$

with
- $\alpha(\omega)$ the attenuation constant.
- $\beta(\omega)$ the phase constant representing the phase shift of the wave over 1 meter of length.
Phase velocity can also be calculated from phase constant to show the transmission distance of wavefront in a unit of time.
Characteristic impedance is the ratio of voltage and current of the wave on the transmission line.
### Reflection Coefficient

$$
\Gamma_V = \dfrac{\rm{Reflected \space wave}}{\rm{Incident \space wave}}
$$


$$
\Gamma_I = - \Gamma_V
$$

When they only mention $\Gamma$, they are refering to $\Gamma_V$.

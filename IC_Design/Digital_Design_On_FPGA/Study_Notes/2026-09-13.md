Setup time is the minimum time the input is required to be stable before the (rising) edge of the clock.
Setup slack is the time difference between actual arrival time and required arrival time.
Hold time is the minimum time the input is required to remain unchanged after the (rising) edge of the clock.
Clock skew is the difference between timing of the same clock edge at different d-flip-flops.
Clock uncertainty is the _total possible time variation between any pair of clock edges_.
Timing closure is the process of ensuring the design meets timing specifications.

Here comes the question: in complex design with multiple timing paths, how do we know the timing of the entire project is satisfied?
Check WNS (Worst Negative Slack), WHS (Worst Hold Slack), TNS (Total Negative Slack), THS (Total Hold Slack). If a design satisfies WNS and WHS, you can rest easy as this ensures that your design has passed timing closure.
## Calculate Maximum Operating Frequency

$$
\mathrm{F_{max}} = \dfrac{1}{\mathrm{T_{min}}}
$$
with $\mathrm{T_{min}}$ the minimum period that the design is still able to satisfy.
If considering a timing path is restricted by setup time:

$$
\mathrm{T_{min}} = \mathrm{T_{constraint}} - WNS
$$

with $\mathrm{T_{constraint}}$ the minimum period that the design is still able to work correctly.
## Determine $\mathrm{T_{min}}$ of Combinational Circuits
$\mathrm{T_{min}}$ is the propagation delay of critical path ($\mathrm{T_{critical}}$). $\mathrm{T_{critical}} = \mathrm{max(data \space path \space delay)}$
We can measure delay of a combination circuit by placing it between pair of d-flip-flops.
For sequential circuit, $\mathrm{T_{min}}$ is data path delay + setup time + clock uncertainty - clock skew.

The basic raw material used in CMOS fabs is a *wafer* or disk of silicon. Wafer size is around 75 to 300 mm in diameter and less than 1 mm thick. Wafers are cut from boules, or cylindrical ingots of single-crystal silicon that are pulled from a crucible of molten silicon. This is Czochralski method, a very common method still in use till today.

At first, a controlled amount of impurities is added to the molten silicon. This gives the crystal its conductive characteristic. A seed is placed in the molten silicon to initiate crystal growth. The silicon ingots take on the same crystal direction as the seed.

Surrounding the crucible is the graphite radiator which is heated up by radiofrequency induction. The temperature is kept at a few degree above the melting point of silicon (1425 $\degree$C). The air is helium or argon to prevent oxidation.

As the seed is being pulled out and rotated, the molten silicon is pulled back and recrystallized as it is withdrawn. The seed withdrawal and rotation rates determine the diameter of the ingot.

The long-channel model is a good approximation in low fields, but *breaks down* when strong lateral or vertical fields are applied.
## Saturation Current Related
Saturation current increases less than quadratically with increasing $V_{gs}$ due to [[Velocity Saturation and Mobility Degradation|velocity saturation and mobility degradation]].
*Velocity degradation* is caused by the carriers tend to hit the lattice more often with increasing field strength. This results in decreasing current.
*Mobility degradation* is caused by high lateral field strength. This makes the carriers scatter off the oxide surface more often, effectively slows them down.
But it increases somewhat with $V_{ds}$ due to [[Channel Length Modulation|channel length modulation]].
## Threshold Voltage Related
Threshold voltage primarily determined by the oxide thickness and channel doping levels. But some other fields contribute to threshold voltage too.
Increase the potential between the source and the body raises the threshold voltage through the [[Body Effect|body effect]].
Increase the drain voltage lowers the threshold through [[Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering|drain-induced barrier lowering]].
Increase the channel length raises the threshold through the [[Short Channel Effect|short channel effect]].
## Leakage Related
*Subthreshold conduction* causes transistor to leak current when it's off $(V_{gs} < V_t)$.
*Gate current leakage* is caused by the gate oxide layers only made up of a few atomic layer so electrons can *tunnel* through the gate.
The junctions between diffusion regions (source and drain) and the body also experience *junction leakage*.

Both mobility and threshold voltage decrease with rising temperature.
Mobility effects are dominated in ON transistors. The higher temperature the less mobility carriers have.
Threshold effects are dominated in OFF transistors. The higher temperature the more leakage the transistor has.
$\Rightarrow$ MOS characteristics degrade with increasing temperature.
